# Recruiting party agents — the pitch

**Audience:** polling agents appointed by political parties, and the party
officials who deploy them.

**Why them:** an agent is at a polling unit by right, watches the count, signs
the result, and is handed a duplicate copy of Form EC8A. They are the only group
in the country that is already present at all ~176,000 units and already holding
the document. Nothing else recruits at that scale.

**The number this exists to fix:** Osun 2026 had **12 organic observers against
3,763 polling units** — 0.3% coverage. See `POSTMORTEM-OSUN-2026.md`. No amount
of engineering compensates for that; only people at units do.

---

## 1. The pitch, in one paragraph

> You already have the result for your polling unit. You watched the count, you
> signed the sheet, you were given a copy. The risk was never at your unit — it
> is what happens to that number between your unit and the final collation. If
> you photograph the sheet through Hawkeye before you leave, your copy is
> timestamped, tied to that location, and published where nobody — including us —
> can alter it afterwards. If the figure that arrives at collation differs from
> the one you signed, you are not arguing from memory or from a photo on your
> phone that anyone can dispute. You have a public record made at the time.

That is the whole product. Everything below answers the objections.

---

## 2. Say the nonpartisan part first, not last

Every agent will think it, so raise it before they do:

> **This works for every party, including the ones you are running against. That
> is exactly why your copy is worth anything.**
>
> A record that only ever published results favourable to one side would be
> worthless as evidence — anyone could dismiss it, and they would be right to.
> Hawkeye publishes what is on the sheet, whoever it favours. That symmetry is
> not a compromise we are asking you to accept; it is the reason a journalist, an
> observer group or a tribunal would take your copy seriously at all.

Two things follow, and both should be said plainly:

- **It protects an honest agent and exposes a dishonest one.** If you are asked
  to sign something that does not match the count, a public record made at the
  unit is the strongest thing you can be holding.
- **You cannot use it selectively.** Publishing only your good units is visible
  in the data. Partial reporting is a pattern, not a secret.

Do not soften this. An agent who signs up expecting a partisan tool will feel
misled later, and that is worse than a smaller number of agents who understood
the deal.

## 3. What it actually asks of them

Four steps, about a minute, on the phone they already carry.

1. Open Hawkeye and pick your polling unit — search by name or code, and the list
   works with no network.
2. Photograph the EC8A sheet you were given.
3. Photograph the polling unit surroundings.
4. Enter the figures as recorded and send.

Free. No data plan beyond the photos. If the network is down, the report queues
and sends itself when signal returns.

## 4. Objections, with honest answers

**"Is this legal?"**
Yes. You are entitled to your copy of the result — you countersigned it and were
given a duplicate under the Electoral Act 2026. Publishing what you were lawfully
given is not an offence, and Hawkeye is explicit on every page that official
results are INEC's alone.

**"Does my party have to approve it?"**
No — it is your own copy of a document you signed. But tell them anyway. An agent
whose party learns about it second-hand is in an awkward position, and a party
that understands it usually wants more agents doing it, not fewer.

**"Does this replace what I send to my party?"**
No. Send your party whatever you normally send. Hawkeye is a second, public copy
that exists outside any party's chain — which is the only reason it can settle a
dispute between two parties' figures.

**"What happens to my phone number?"**
It is used to create the account and is never published. What is published is the
result, the polling unit, the time, and the photographs of the sheet. Not you.

**"What if the figures are challenged later?"**
That is the point. Every submission is timestamped, tied to the unit's location,
and anchored in a public transparency log, so the record can be shown to be
unaltered since the moment it was made. That is a stronger position than a photo
in a WhatsApp thread.

**"Is it safe for me?"**
Report after the count, from wherever you are — you do not have to stand there
doing it. Never argue with anyone about a result. Step away first; no figure is
worth your safety.

**"What if I make a mistake?"**
Enter what the sheet says, not what you think it should say. Where several agents
at the same unit report the same numbers, confidence rises; where they differ,
the unit is flagged for review rather than quietly averaged.

## 5. What we ask, and when

The Osun post-mortem is blunt about where this fails: people sign up and then do
not report. Timing is most of the fix.

**Before election day**
- Sign up now, not at the polling unit. One code, about a minute — and the
  network at a unit on the day is the worst it will be all week.
- Save the unit you will be posted to.
- Do one **practice run**. It is a full rehearsal that publishes nothing.

**On the day**
- Report at poll-close, when the sheet is posted and you have your copy.
- If you also serve at collation, report the collated figures too.

## 6. For party leadership — the shorter case

- **Coverage is the whole game.** Your agents are already deployed; this asks
  them for one extra minute at the point they are already holding the sheet.
- **An independent record helps a genuine petition.** Figures published from the
  unit at the time, by an organisation with no stake in the outcome, are harder
  to wave away than your own agents' returns alone.
- **It is symmetric, and we will not pretend otherwise.** Every other party can
  do the same, and the record will show whoever it shows. If your case is that
  the count was fair to you, this is the cheapest way to prove it.
- **No cost, no exclusivity, no data-sharing agreement.** We do not sell,
  transfer or licence observer data to any party.

---

## 7. What Hawkeye does not do

Say these unprompted; the credibility is worth more than the sign-up.

- It does not declare results. INEC does. Everything published is explicitly
  unofficial and labelled as crowd-reported.
- It does not offer legal advice or represent anyone in a petition.
- It does not pay agents, and it will not — payment would make every report
  suspect.
- It cannot stop anything happening at a unit. It records what was there.
